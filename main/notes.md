## Our contribution
- Q1. "Through what specific molecular mechanism does a single DNA letter change actually cause the brain to start 'clogging the pipes'?"
  - We are testing the hypothesis that non-coding risk variants act as the physical "volume knobs" (eQTLs) that turn production up.
  - An eQTL is just a statistical correlation. It tells: "People with an 'A' here usually have more APP protein." It does not tell how it happened.
  - It is going to uncover the middle steps.
 
- Q2. "Which specific DNA typos cause the cell to mis-edit the Tau protein, leading to the collapse of the neuron's internal transport system?"
  - In Alzheimer’s, mutations in the MAPT gene act as a "bad editor" (sQTLs), causing the cell to "cut and paste" instructions incorrectly.
  - what they know:
    - Scientists have discovered rare, specific mutations in the MAPT gene that are "causal," meaning they virtually guarantee a person will develop a
      neurodegenerative disease (usually Frontotemporal Dementia).
  - What they don't know: 
    - Research knows that "Risk Variants" identified in GWAS make people vulnerable, but they do not know exactly which specific DNA typos act as the
      "bad editor" (sQTL).
- Q3. "Does the APOE4 risk variant create a 'clunky' environment by changing the regulatory availability of the brain's garbage collection system?"
  -  We will test if the APOE4 variant shows a different DNASE or TF Delta compared to the "shielding" APOE2 version. This answers whether the risk is
    just the protein's shape, or if the cell is also failing to produce enough of the "cleaner" protein in the first place
  - What they know:
    - Research establishes that the APOE protein acts as a "garbage collector" for amyloid-beta. The APOE4 variant is known to be "clunky,"
    binding to amyloid less effectively than the APOE3 or APOE2 models. This leads to amyloid staying in the brain longer and forming plaques.
  - What they don't know:
    - Is the failure only due to the shape of the protein? Research has not yet mapped whether the APOE4 variant also acts as a regulatory
    "volume knob" (eQTL) that reduces the total amount of "garbage collector" trucks the cell is allowed to build.

- Q4. "Can we identify 'Hidden Switches' in people who don't have rare mutations but still develop pathological 'biological smog'?"
  - Most people with Alzheimer's do not have rare mutations in APP or MAPT; they have "Risk Variants" like APOE4 that make them vulnerable.
  - Through Dense ISM, we are looking for previously undiscovered "high-delta" hits. This answers why someone without a family history of AD
    might still produce enough Amyloid to "clog the pipes".
  - What they know:
    - Most Alzheimer's patients do not have rare, causal mutations in the $APP$ gene. Instead, sticky fragments called $A\beta_{42}$
    clump into "biological smog" (plaques) outside neurons. Making too much $APP$ is known to be as dangerous as having a mutated version of the gene.
  - What they don't know:
    - Where are the "volume knobs" for the $99\%$ of patients without rare mutations? GWAS finds the general "risky neighborhood,"
    But it cannot identify the exact base pair that turns $APP$ production up.

- Q5. "Which genetic variants trigger the brain's immune cells to shift from 'cleaners' to 'attackers'? Activating the panic mode."
  - Microglia (immune cells) try to clean amyloid clumps but can get overwhelmed and start attacking healthy neurons by mistake.
  - APOE4 is known to trigger these immune cells to stay in a "panic mode," causing chronic inflammation.
  - By testing variants associated with Neuroinflammation, we are looking for the "switches" that turn a healthy immune response into a self-destructive one.
  - What they know:
    - Microglia are the brain's immune cells. In Alzheimer's, they shift from "cleaners" to "attackers," attacking healthy neurons by mistake. It is established that
      APOE4 specifically triggers these cells to stay in a "panic mode," causing the chronic inflammation that kills neurons.
  - What they don't know:
    - What is the physical genetic trigger for this "panic mode"? Biological research has not identified the specific regulatory switches that tell a microglial cell to
      stop cleaning and start attacking.
